Research

I study how interactions and fluctuations shape the collective behavior of physical systems. My work combines analytical theory with numerical calculations, from ionic solutions and soft materials to random networks.

Ionic transport & correlations

How do interacting ions respond to an electric field? I use stochastic density functional theory and fluctuating hydrodynamics to study electrolyte conductivity and the evolution of ion–ion correlations.

My work examines the response to fields that are switched on, switched off, or oscillate in time. It connects the microscopic organization of the ions to the macroscopic electrical current.

Glass transition in polymer films

Thin polymer films can have a glass-transition temperature that differs markedly from the bulk material. I studied a proposed explanation based on the motion of chain segments connecting the two free surfaces.

Using confined random walks and first-passage statistics, we calculated the properties of these polymer bridges and tested the proposed mechanism against experimental observations.

Microscopic origins of granular flow

How do interactions between individual grains determine the flow of a granular material? I analyzed discrete-element simulation results and contributed to a theoretical scaling description of dense granular flows.

This collaborative work connects grain-scale velocity fluctuations and transient clusters to macroscopic friction and flow laws.

Cycles & structure in random networks

The local structure of a network affects how it connects, transmits signals, and responds to the removal of nodes or edges. I studied shortest cycles, articulation points, and bridge edges in random-network ensembles.

Using generating functions, cavity methods, and numerical graph ensembles, we derived statistical descriptions of these structural features and compared the predictions with simulations.
